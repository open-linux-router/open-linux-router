# World traffic

Overview's Traffic card flips to a MapLibre preview with a bundled Natural
Earth 1:110m country map (public domain). Opening fullscreen loads a separate,
fingerprinted local GeoJSON asset with 1:50m countries and simplified global
1:10m first-order administrative boundaries, label points and major cities.
State/province boundaries appear from zoom 2; their names and capital/world-city
names start at zoom 3. Million-plus cities appear from zoom 4 and the remaining
major cities from zoom 5, with collision handling and population priority.
Administrative label thresholds are brought forward for this regional view
rather than copied from a street basemap; integer thresholds match MapLibre's
symbol layout evaluation. Zoom stops at 6: this is an
administrative traffic map, without roads or buildings. The fullscreen view is
map only: no title, legend, status, credit or other text overlaps it, and the
single floating close button at top right is its only control. It has its own
/map address, so a link or bookmark opens the map directly. Pan, pinch, scroll
and double-click zoom stand alone. Resizing preserves the chosen
fullscreen view. MapLibre renders labels with local system fonts; there are
no external map, font, sprite or tile requests, including on first use.
The asset ships inside OLR and uses the existing immutable asset cache.

Only the top forty IPs are located using cached ip.sb city lookups; unknown
locations remain in the ranking and its coverage denominator. Coordinates
are approximate IP locations, not physical device positions. Arcs use only
valid returned coordinates, without GeoIP city, region or country labels or
country-center fallbacks. IPs without coordinates remain in the ranking but
are not plotted. Administrative label points are representative, not IP locations.

The detailed dataset is generated by `web/scripts/build-region-map.mjs` from
Natural Earth v5.1.2's `ne_50m_admin_0_countries.geojson`,
`ne_10m_admin_1_states_provinces_lines.geojson`,
`ne_10m_admin_1_states_provinces.geojson`, and
`ne_10m_populated_places_simple.geojson`. Cities are limited to Natural Earth
scale ranks 0–5 plus national capitals. Pass a directory containing those
files to the script. Geometry is simplified at 0.02 degrees and rounded to
three decimal places, unused properties and province polygons are discarded,
and internal boundary segments are grouped by country. Regeneration is an
explicit maintenance step; normal builds and runtime never download data.
See the [source dataset](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/tree/v5.1.2/geojson)
and [public-domain terms](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/).

Country fills use MapLibre's demo palette in light mode and muted equivalents
in dark mode. Each country has a stable palette index generated from the
country adjacency graph, preferring Natural Earth's MAPCOLOR8 assignment.
Shared vertices in both source and simplified geometry determine adjacency,
including corner contacts. Deterministic saturation-degree coloring resolves
conflicts and regeneration verifies all detected neighbors have different
indices. Dataset order and traffic volume do not affect country colors.

The daemon samples IPv4/IPv6 conntrack counters every two seconds, shared
across browsers. It retains counter differences over up to thirty seconds
in memory, aggregates by remote IP, and selects forty by combined upload and
download. Ranking has deterministic IP tie-breaking. Rates are bytes/second
over the available window; the first sample is a baseline. Coverage is the
top forty's share of all observed eligible remote-IP traffic, not a share of
the separate nftables interface/device totals.

Blue indicates download and teal upload in the details list. The map itself
uses one amber hue for every arc, deliberately outside the teal-green-blue
country palette, so colour marks traffic as a category. Every arc is 2px and
keeps that weight for as long as its location reports traffic in the sampled
window; an arc fades out only when the location leaves the window — no eligible
bytes in the last thirty seconds, or pushed below the top forty. A line that
dimmed while its flow continued would claim that a connection had ended, which
the sampler cannot know: it differences conntrack counters every two seconds,
keeps thirty seconds of deltas, and reports a rate per remote IP. It observes no
connection start or end, so appearance and disappearance mean "in the window"
and "out of it", not "opened" and "closed". Width carries no volume either; the
ranked rates live in the details dialog, and the map stays a presence and
direction picture. A blurred amber halo sits under each arc; because arcs
composite per feature, the halo accumulates where they bunch up — around the
router and along busy corridors — and concentration reads as light. Every arc
stays translucent so overlaps build up instead of replacing each other, and
width is a single 2px at the world view that thins with zoom, so a country-sized
view is not covered by ribbons meant for a globe.
A highlight rides a few arcs at a time, not all of them: the light is a short
stretch of the path drawn as two blurred strokes with a tail-to-head gradient,
pale warm light rather than white so it lifts the amber without becoming a
second hue. It travels out and back in four seconds each way and fades near both
ends, so the light never lands at the hub or on a destination. The number of
concurrent lights is a quarter of the plotted arcs, from one to five; a turn
lasts about six seconds, a location that has just appeared goes to the front of
the queue, and the rest are ordered by fewest turns and longest wait, so every
arc is served within a minute. Each arc keeps its own phase, so concurrent lights
sit at different points along their lines. Both views draw the same rotation; the
card preview has room for a soft streak where a bead used to be a speck.
Reduced motion drops the highlight and interpolation. The UI polls every two
seconds and keeps each IP's highlight phase independent of ranking. Paths fade
in and out over a fixed 450ms eased curve, so a fade always finishes.
A snapshot can add or drop several locations at once, so those fades are spread
across a window that closes before the next snapshot: each location owns a slot
in the window and starts at a random point inside it, which keeps the gaps
irregular instead of a metronome. The window is capped at 1.35 seconds (the two
second poll minus the fade and a margin), so even a full batch has settled
before the next one arrives, and a lone change still starts at once. The
staggering is presentation only: a location is in or out of the window at the
moment its snapshot says so, and every fade is the same length either way.
Visual updates render at up to 30fps, and hidden tabs pause them. The highlight
stays within amber and warm light, so amber is the only hue the map adds. There
are no endpoint dots: the arcs carry the whole reading, and the hub is where
they converge.
This interpolation visualizes sampled activity; it is not a packet trace.
Different returned locations within one country can have an arc; a location at
the router's own coordinates gets no arc. The details dialog
exposes IPs and both rates, unavailable states, sample duration and GeoIP attribution.

Gateway traffic counting enables `net.netfilter.nf_conntrack_acct=1` through
the normal apply plan. This affects newly created connections; older entries
may lack counters until they expire. Turning counting off stops this sampler
and clears its window, but does not disable the shared kernel facility used
by remote access. No connection metadata is persisted by this sampler.

Measurements include router-originated and forwarded connections with one
local endpoint and one public remote endpoint. Private, link-local and CGNAT
destinations are excluded. Conntrack original/reply counters are translated
according to endpoint ownership, including inbound connections. Short-lived
connections that disappear between samples, final bytes of expired flows,
and traffic bypassing kernel accounting can be missed. VPN endpoints are
displayed as observed; inner destinations are not inferred.

Large connection tables still cost a full netlink dump, parsing and memory
per sample. Measure this on target hardware before reducing the interval.
The map's top-ten coverage does not claim exact whole-router accounting.
